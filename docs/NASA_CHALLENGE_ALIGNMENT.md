# NASA Space Apps challenge alignment

## Source review

The official challenge is [Identify Earth Locations that Analog the Permanent Moon Base Locations and Mars](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2026/challenges/permanent-moon-base-locations-and-mars/). The challenge summary describes using open Earth, Moon, and Mars data to identify and characterize terrestrial analogues for future bases or landing sites, considering settings including deserts, polar regions, volcanic and arid terrains, and caves. It calls for connecting Earth settings to lunar or Martian surface conditions and presenting findings in a map, visualization, or other tool.

The supplied Details/Resources URL was requested during this work. The site’s public page returned an incomplete client-rendered response with no Details or Resources content, and the web reader returned a fetch error. Therefore, this document treats the accessible challenge summary as the evidence and does not infer additional formal judging requirements. Recheck the Details and Resources tabs when they become accessible.

## Interpretation for Phase 0

This phase tests one enabling part of the broader challenge: whether image retrieval can help discover small-scale Earth–Mars dune morphology candidates across geographically independent regions. It does not identify landing sites, evaluate habitability, compare the Moon, or present a site-selection map. A useful result would be a transparent list of candidates for deeper scientific review, together with counterexamples and uncertainty.

The challenge summary emphasizes characterization and multiple environmental factors. Consequently, visual similarity alone cannot establish an analogue. Promising pairs require independent terrain, geological, compositional, or process evidence, and an explicit account of where the analogy fails. Exploration relevance should be site-specific (for example, testing sediment-transport interpretations or observation strategies), not a generic claim that any desert prepares missions.

## Limitations

- The challenge page exposes only its summary to the available retrieval path; linked resource material and tab-specific content were not verified.
- A dune-first Earth–Mars benchmark is narrower than the challenge’s lunar/Martian site and environment scope.
- The benchmark’s image-retrieval metrics cannot rank operational landing sites or establish geological equivalence.
- Results and conclusions must be based on acquired observations and independent review; target sample counts are not evidence that the data exist.
