# ENOM Data Usage Audit

This audit describes what ENOM actually has connected in its code and local data. A source link in the app is not proof that measurements from that source are being used.

## Current coverage

ENOM's place-based discovery currently searches **19 places in its checked-in catalogue**: 8 Earth, 7 Moon, and 4 Mars. This includes two separate older Mare Tranquillitatis map contexts at different coordinates. They are kept separate because they cover different areas. These are not a search of every place on Earth or another world.

Catalogue entries marked `archived` and image groups with no reviewed labels are hidden from the default discovery results. The records remain in the catalogue/manifests and can be included for audit or restored by changing their catalogue status. In mixed image groups, unreviewed image patches are not attached to the default place view.

The app ranks up to 10 distinct Earth places against a Moon or Mars target. It reports the best matches within the current catalogue. A missing measurement is shown as missing; it is not treated as a score of zero.

## How evidence reaches the interface

1. **Ingestion:** scripts or checked-in sidecar files describe local raster windows, map queries, climate records, or literature studies. Large original source archives are not copied wholesale into ENOM.
2. **Place linking:** a stable catalogue place has an ID. Its images and source observations are attached to it. Nearby observed contexts are grouped only when their centres are within 5 km; different windows farther apart remain separate places.
3. **Processing:** terrain code derives height range, slope, local relief, and roughness from valid DEM cells. Coverage masks and source metadata remain attached. Literature and map interpretations are shown as contextual evidence, not converted into measurements.
4. **Evidence API:** the discovery API returns the source, product, location, spatial and time coverage, units, uncertainty notes, evidence type, and use limitations by dimension. It also lists missing sources and coverage gaps.
5. **Ranking:** the API compares compatible terrain measurements between a planetary target and Earth places. It also uses a separate terrain-operations proxy based on compatible slope, local-relief, and roughness measurements. The frontend shows each dimension's score or why it cannot be scored, evidence coverage, and up to 10 distinct places.
6. **Maps and evidence:** elevation, slope, roughness, coverage, imagery, thermal context, geology context, bounded Mini-RF CPR samples, and Jezero rover position fixes can be inspected as source layers. Elevation and slope can be combined only when both use the same source DEM grid. Diviner PRP and LEND measurements appear as place-linked evidence records. Earth and planetary maps stay in their own coordinate systems and are shown side by side; they are not falsely overlaid on one shared coordinate grid.

## What changes the ranking today?

Two dimensions currently affect analogue ranking: **terrain** and a **terrain-operations proxy**. Both compare source-derived measurements only when names, units, and spatial scales match within 5%. The operations proxy uses slope, local relief, and roughness to describe terrain that may matter to landing or mobility. The same DEM can support both dimensions, so users can intentionally give terrain hazards extra importance through the profile weights. Missing, unavailable, or incompatible measurements are left out, not scored as zero; evidence coverage is reported alongside each score.

The app has editable profiles for dunes, polar resources, geology/materials, subsurface targets, and mission operations. Their weights are stored and returned by the API. Weights for the other six dimensions cannot yet change numeric rankings because those dimensions do not have validated cross-body scoring methods connected. If neither terrain nor the terrain-operations proxy can be compared, the candidate has no numeric rank.

The app keeps **analogue similarity** separate from **mission suitability**. The terrain-operations proxy is only a comparison of DEM-derived terrain variables; it does not check vehicle-specific limits, the full hazard environment, or a mission plan. Discovery still reports mission suitability as not assessed. A separate legacy scorecard can check a limited set of terrain values against provisional engineering thresholds, but those thresholds are not spacecraft requirements and do not establish that a real site is safe.

## Scientific limits

- **Different instruments measure different things.** Diviner surface temperature, TES thermal inertia, ERA5/POWER air temperature, and solar radiation cannot be treated as one interchangeable thermal variable. ERA5 is a roughly 25 km Earth reanalysis grid, so its nearest grid point can be kilometres from a candidate location.
- **A common grid does not make data equally accurate.** Copernicus is a surface model; MOLA and LOLA are planetary elevation products. Their original resolution, sampling, interpolation, and error differ.
- **Coverage is incomplete.** Sparse MOLA observations can miss local high or low points. The LOLA polar crops now cover the three candidate locator points, but each is a small region rather than a landing ellipse or traverse; its count grid describes observation support, not elevation error. Candidate imagery, DEMs, and geology maps do not all cover the same footprints.
- **Map context is not a field measurement.** Regional geology, spectral-library entries, literature studies, and remote-sensing resource indicators have different spatial precision and meaning. A possible pit is not a confirmed accessible cave; a remote signal is not proof of usable water or ice.
- **Mini-RF is not an ice confirmation.** The connected CPR values summarize small neighbourhoods in a roughly 948 m per pixel polar mosaic. The product has no per-pixel uncertainty, and high CPR can have non-ice explanations. ENOM shows the values as clues only; LEND is another indirect measure, not ground truth or independent confirmation of usable ice.
- **LEND is indirect and spatially broad.** ENOM connects the 0.5° south-polar ALDS grid cells and retains the published CSETN statistical error. The map cell and neutron instrument footprint are larger than a landing spot; the error does not capture all location, footprint, or systematic uncertainty. A missing cell is shown as unavailable, not as zero or evidence that hydrogen is absent.
- **Diviner PRP is a model, not an ice map.** ENOM samples the nearest ~0.5 km triangle for each of three lunar south-polar candidates. Annual temperature and ice-stability depth are thermal-model estimates fitted to the first mapping year, not current conditions or direct ice detections. The archive supplies no per-triangle uncertainty; `-999` means the modeled stability depth is beyond 2.8738 m and is shown as not reported. These lunar-only values are not compared with Earth's air temperature or included in rankings.
- **Rover positions do not describe every wheel movement.** The Jezero subset uses PDS PLACES Release 16 best-tactical positions attached to drive records. ENOM shows 577 discrete fixes within 20 km of its catalogue coordinate, through sol 1896. Release 16 says `ROVER,n,0` rows are missing from this table; those starts are represented by `SITE` rows, which ENOM excludes from its drive fixes. The source table does not provide per-position uncertainty in this ingest, and the fixes do not show exact paths between positions, wheel performance, or safe routes. They are operations evidence only and do not affect rankings or mission suitability.
- **Uncertainty is mostly described, not propagated.** Source limitations and some measurement uncertainty notes are shown, but ENOM does not combine instrument error, location error, resampling, time variation, and systematic dataset bias into a total uncertainty range.
- **The profiles are ahead of the connected scoring science.** Six dimensions are visible and can hold evidence, but their weights do not yet influence numeric ranks. The operations proxy reuses terrain measures and is not a substitute for vehicle engineering or operations records.
- **MEDA is a small sample.** The current Jezero connection summarizes one sol. It shows actual rover observations, but cannot describe Jezero's seasonal climate or be compared directly with NASA POWER's Earth climatology. PDS humidity documentation warns that continuous-mode self-heating affects some readings; ENOM retains this caveat and does not score the data.
- **Candidate discovery is catalogue-limited.** ENOM ranks places already in its candidate catalogue; it does not algorithmically discover every suitable place on Earth or another body.

## Prioritised recommendations

1. **Add compatible measured evidence before expanding the scoring formula.** Start with one question-specific dimension at a time. For example, connect source-backed mineral observations at paired candidate sites before letting geology weights affect a rank.
2. **Extend polar terrain detail and quality checks.** The first LOLA 120 m south-polar windows and direct-shot counts are connected at Shackleton, Malapert, and de Gerlache. Compare them against the higher-resolution polar products where their size and coverage allow, retain source-support masks, and avoid treating the candidate locator as a landing ellipse.
3. **Connect higher-resolution terrain and hazards.** Add local HiRISE stereo products for Mars and an appropriate Earth elevation product for candidate sites; calculate slope and roughness at clearly stated scales.
4. **Add comparable thermal and illumination evidence.** Keep surface temperature, air temperature, thermal inertia, and illumination as separate variables. Record time of day, season, spatial scale, and derivation method.
5. **Strengthen resource evidence with independent sources.** Mini-RF CPR and LEND neutron rates are now connected as context at three lunar south-polar candidates. Next compare their different footprints and uncertainty carefully, then connect Mars SHARAD/CRISM and Earth NSIDC products with product-specific coverage. Keep all remote detections labeled as clues or estimates, not confirmed usable resources.
6. **Connect process and operations records.** Add repeat imagery and rover weather for dust/change evidence, surveyed Earth cave records and planetary subsurface clues, and mission records tied to a named vehicle and location.
7. **Improve uncertainty and compatibility checks.** Track source resolution, native units, spatial and temporal overlap, interpolation, uncertainty, and evidence quality. Exclude incompatible observations with a visible reason.
8. **Expand candidate coverage after source coverage is reliable.** The current top 10 means the best matches among the 19 indexed places. A broader algorithmic search needs a larger, consistently measured place catalogue first.
